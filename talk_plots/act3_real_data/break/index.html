Where and when does the part crack?

Part abc_00000065 crushed in an Instron. Real first crack: the first load drop, at the peak, 4,755 N at 2.51 mm crosshead, at the lower inner bore (observed by the team on the broken part). The simulation has no fracture model, so this is a crack-onset criterion checked on each CalculiX increment: the largest tension s1 reaching the resin's strength (50.5 MPa: predicted crack) or 85% of it (42.9 MPa: potential crack), or the plastic strain PEEQ reaching the dogbone's strain at break (0.043).

Answer

  • After post-training (most accurate mesh): no break predicted by the full-strength criterion up to 2.78 mm, but a potential crack at 2.51 mm, 4,814 N (real crack: 2.51 mm, 4,755 N), on the lower inner bore (f5): the place where the part actually cracked. There the tension is 43 MPa at the real crack, 85% of the strength.
  • Before post-training: crack predicted at 1.76 mm, 3,162 N (potential crack already at 1.50 mm), 0.75 mm and 1,593 N before the real crack, and in the wrong place: on the upper inner edge of a prong (f13 / f16 (flat side, prong inner side)), where the frictionless platens let the prong tops slide inward (about 6 mm each by the end) and bend the prongs. The real crack is at the lower inner bore.
  • The strain criterion fires far too early in every case (1.0-1.6 mm raw), always at the nut-pocket corners (f8 with f38/f39 or f41/f42). There the resin is squeezed from all sides (after: s1 = -53 MPa, s3 = -113 MPa when PEEQ passes 0.043), so a tensile break strain does not apply; and PEEQ at a sharp corner keeps growing as the mesh is refined.

At the real crack every mesh puts the largest tension at 42-49 MPa (83-98 % of the strength); only the pre-trained swarm mesh (2.58 mm) and the uniform 2 mm mesh (2.71 mm) cross it, just after the real crack. Where the 85 % tier is first reached moves with the mesh: the lower inner bore on the post-trained, 4 mm and 2.5 mm meshes, a nut-pocket corner on the swarm and 2 mm meshes, the outer side on the coarse default mesh.

Caveats

  • Onset only: a criterion read off a continuum simulation; no crack, no crack growth, no load drop is modelled.
  • The 85% tier was chosen after seeing this test, so its timing match is not an independent prediction.
  • One dogbone gives both thresholds; its strain is from the crosshead (includes grip compliance), and the 50.5 MPa is an engineering stress while s1 is a true (Cauchy) stress: the true strength is a few % higher, which would push the stress-criterion onset later still.
  • Nodal s1 and PEEQ are recovered from integration points; at sharp re-entrant corners both depend on the mesh (PEEQ strongly), which is why the robust values and the mesh table are given.
  • The real crack site (lower inner bore, face f5) is the team's observation of the broken part; there is no photo in the repo, and which side and the exact point were not recorded: the black ring is drawn at the simulation's most stressed bore point, so the match is a region match, not a point match.
  • The printed part may break earlier than the bulk resin (layer lines, voids, the sharp pocket corners); the test cracked at 4,755 N where the best simulation is at 85% of the strength, which is within that kind of scatter.
