# Anchor meshing agents in kernel-computed geometry

The literature through October 2026 says three things. First, frontier models understand CAD best through **code and structured text, not through images or raw B-rep**. On BenchCAD, the same models answer numeric questions about a part at about **0.84 when given CadQuery code but only about 0.59 when given renders**. Second, models still cannot reliably name or pick the specific faces and edges a meshing workflow depends on. LLM-written face selectors reach only **21.51% F1**. Learned B-rep encoders score about 99% on synthetic benchmarks but "collapse catastrophically" when the same solid is re-exported. No published work reasons over raw STEP text, and none converts a STEP B-rep into exact, editable feature code. Code reconstruction from meshes is approximate: IoU runs from about 65% to 97% depending on part complexity. Third, on the simulation side, 2026 evidence shows that a **single generic coding-agent harness with an execution-feedback repair loop and tutorial retrieval beats bespoke multi-agent CFD systems (96.4% vs 88.2% on FoamBench)**. Yet meshing of novel geometry is the weakest stage in every published agent, none checks mesh quality quantitatively, and no meshing benchmark exists. This report therefore recommends a Claude Code-style harness that keeps the STEP file as the lossless source of truth. A deterministic OCCT layer gives the model a token-budgeted "geometry digest", predicate-based face queries and stable semantic names. The mesh is expressed as code: a re-runnable gmsh Python script driven by a declarative spec. The loop is closed by hook-enforced mesh tests (validity, geometric fidelity, named groups, element quality, solver acceptance, grid convergence) plus quality-coloured renders. With that design, verification rigor becomes the main point of difference, because neither published agents nor commercial products (Ansys Mesh Agent, the SimScale Onshape agent) report it.

## Models read CAD code at 0.84 but renders at 0.59

### CadQuery became the default text form of CAD

Since 2025 the field has largely settled on **CadQuery (Python on OpenCASCADE)** as the textual CAD representation. The evidence for that choice is direct. Text2CAD-Bench compared DeepCAD-style command sequences with CadQuery across models. Command sequences produced higher invalidity rates for nearly all of them, because Python "exploit[s] pretrained code generation capabilities." Some command-sequence metrics looked better only because the survivors were the easy cases ([Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1)). General frontier LLMs writing CadQuery now beat the older specialist models by a wide margin. On Text2CAD-Bench L1 prompts, GPT-5.2 reaches a Chamfer distance of 44.31. The command-sequence models Text2CAD and CADFusion land at 219.57 and 229.62: they "execute but are geometrically wrong" ([Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1)). On simple, well-specified parts the problem is now solved. The 25-task CadQueryEval was archived in September 2026 as saturated, with **claude-opus-5.5, gpt-6-sol and others at 100%**. Its small size gives a standard error of about 0.1 ([cadqueryeval](https://github.com/danwahl/cadqueryeval)).

The competence is uneven. On BenchCAD, frontier models edit existing CadQuery well: GPT-5.3 scores 0.865 and Opus 4.7 0.853. But **about 64% of nominally successful edits silently corrupt unrelated features**, and edits specified by a target render instead of text drop every model to near zero ([BenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10865v1)). Image-to-code is where frontier models are weakest. Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Opus 4.7 score about 0.38–0.40 on img2cq, while a 2B Qwen3-VL fine-tuned with RL scores 0.768 ([BenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10865v1)). Advanced operations also break generation. On Text2CAD-Bench L3 (sweeps, lofts, shells), GPT-5.2 fails to produce valid geometry 68% of the time and Claude-4.5-Sonnet 70%. Executability, geometric similarity and feature quality "do not track each other" ([Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1)).

For an agent that must *understand* a part rather than create it, the most useful result is the gap between modalities. **BenchCAD code QA tops out near 0.84; vision QA tops out near 0.59**, against blank-input baselines of 0.22 and 0.38 ([BenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10865v1)). An earlier result points the same way. GPT-4o answered 76.5% of SGP-Bench CAD questions when given CAD-Recode's reconstructed CadQuery, against 42.3% for PointLLM reading the raw points ([CAD-Recode](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14042v2)). Text that names the geometry explicitly beats pixels that make the model infer it.

### Topology references are the hole in code understanding

The documented failure modes fall exactly where meshing needs precision. Models build correct shapes on the wrong workplane. They swap sweeps for extrusions, drop threads and chamfers, approximate countable features such as gear teeth instead of counting them, and work around early wrong-plane errors instead of undoing them ([BenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10865v1); [CADENA](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00799)). The decisive number comes from FutureCAD. An LLM writing CadQuery selector code to pick the faces and edges an instruction refers to reached **21.51% F1**. A dedicated B-rep grounding GNN reached 50.23%, and the authors blamed "the inability of LLMs to perceive B-Rep geometry" ([FutureCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11831v2)). For meshing, this is the step that maps "the inlet," "the bolted face" or "the fillet at the root" onto actual B-rep entities. **The model must not compute face selections itself; it must call deterministic queries and reason over their output.**

### Reverse-engineering STEP into code is approximate and should stay advisory

Reconstruction research turns geometry back into CadQuery, but every system starts from tessellated or sampled geometry, never from B-rep topology. On DeepCAD and Fusion 360 the task is close to saturated: CADENA reaches GMS 97.0 and 93.3 with near-zero invalidity, and cadrille reaches 92.2% IoU from multi-view images ([CADENA](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00799); [cadrille](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v3)). Fidelity falls on real and industrial parts. CADENA scores GMS 73.7 on MCB and 67.0 on its own industrial benchmark. cadrille reaches 65.0% IoU on CC3D scans, and CAD-Recode handles only sketch-extrude, failing on revolves, fillets and B-splines ([CADENA](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00799); [cadrille](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v3); [CAD-Recode](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14042v2)). CADENA's authors also warn that exported programs "may not transfer cleanly to industrial CAD," and its weakest families are gears, bearings, springs and fasteners. Those are common in real assemblies. No paper converts a STEP B-rep into exact feature-tree code. **For a meshing agent handed someone else's STEP file, reconstructed code is therefore a semantic aid, never a replacement for the B-rep that gets meshed.**

The feedback-loop literature carries over directly. The largest, best-quantified gains come from executing the code and comparing the result to a geometric target. CAD-Recode's best-of-10 selection by Chamfer distance cuts invalidity from 4.9% to 0.4% on DeepCAD and from 16.8% to 0.3% on CC3D ([CAD-Recode](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14042v2)). cadrille's online RL with an IoU reward lifts CC3D IoU from 61.8 to 67.9 ([cadrille](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v3)). CADReasoner's iterative render-overlay self-editing cuts MCB invalidity from 21.9% to 4.0% over five rounds ([CADReasoner](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29847v1)). Pure VLM self-verification helps less. CADCodeVerify's yes/no visual answers were correct only **64.6–68.2%** of the time, and an oracle geometric solver beat it on point-cloud distance, 0.103 vs 0.127 ([CADCodeVerify](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05340v2)). A meshing agent with a STEP input already holds the ground truth, so the strongest loop type costs it almost nothing. It can compare volume, area, bounding box and Hausdorff distance between source CAD, any modified geometry, and the mesh.

## B-rep encoders hit 99% on synthetic parts and collapse on re-exported ones

### Learned B-rep recognition is strong in the lab and brittle in the wild

Native B-rep networks recognize machining features almost perfectly on synthetic data. AAGNet scores about 99.29% on MFCAD++ and BRT 99.26% ([BRT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07134)). On the realistic Fusion 360 Gallery task of labeling which modeling operation produced each face, accuracy drops to about 89–90% and IoU to about 66–70%. That holds for UV-Net, BRepNet and BRT ([BRT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07134); [BRepNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00706v1)). A September 2026 study matters most for an agent that ingests other people's files. One solid has many valid B-reps depending on modeling choices, kernel rebuilds and export settings, and **popular encoders "often collapse catastrophically" under such variation**. A canonical region graph derived from the solid itself fixes this ([Learn the Solid, Not the File](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11573)). Sample efficiency is improving. FeatureFox reaches panoptic quality above 0.9 with about 250 training parts, where AAGNet needs about 5,000 ([FeatureFox](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.26770)). That matters because meshing-specific labels will be scarce. The recognized classes (holes, slots, fillets, chamfers, bosses) map well onto defeaturing candidates; Hierarchical CADNet and MFCAD++ were built explicitly to automate defeaturing for analysis ([QUB thesis](https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/studentTheses/deep-learning-for-boundary-representation-cad-models/)). But **no benchmark labels faces with simulation semantics** such as inlet, outlet, wall, load surface or thin-wall region.

### Kernel-computed summaries beat raw STEP, by default rather than by experiment

Three ways of feeding B-rep to LLMs appear in the literature: learned encoders projected into token space, kernel-computed structured summaries, and raw STEP or CAD code. BrepLLM projects a hierarchical B-rep encoder into Qwen3-8B. It beats multi-view and point-cloud LLMs on its own captioning and classification set, but by modest margins (61.36 vs 56.58 captioning, 60.0% vs 56.25% classification), with no text-serialization baseline and an LLM judge ([BrepLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16413)). STEP-LLM shows that models can emit valid STEP syntax (95.18% renderable), which says nothing about reasoning over existing files ([STEP-LLM](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.12641)). The working agent precedent is AgentsCAD (CMU, July 2026). It notes that raw STEP "encodes thousands of faces, edges, and vertices" in a form LLMs "cannot parse directly." It uses CadQuery to give a Claude Sonnet agent **per-face summaries (surface type, area, normal, centroid) plus face adjacency**, from which the model infers features such as through-holes. Its evaluation is a single qualitative case study, and the authors call the problem only "partially solved" ([AgentsCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02448)). **No controlled study compares raw STEP, JSON face graphs, natural-language summaries and CadQuery for LLM question answering on existing parts.** The structured-summary choice rests on convergent indirect evidence, not a head-to-head result.

### Spatial reasoning improves when structure is handed over

Frontier VLMs remain mediocre at reading mechanical parts from images. On the 85-question CAD-VQA set, Claude-3.5-Sonnet scored 61%, GPT-4o 54% and o1-preview 42% ([CAD-VQA](https://github.com/asgsaeid/cad_vqa)). The 2026 benchmarks find the same weak points. OmniCAD reports above 90% accuracy at identifying non-repeated parts but **about 26% for repeated parts**, and supplying the mate graph "significantly improved" pose estimation ([OmniCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22637)). MechVQA likewise finds MLLMs leaning on unreliable spatial-relation reasoning ([MechVQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30794v1)). Both lessons apply to a geometry digest. Repeated entities such as bolt-hole patterns and fin arrays must be explicitly grouped and pattern-labeled. Computed relations (adjacency, coaxiality, symmetry, distances) should be given to the model, not left for it to infer.

### Meshing-grade geometry analysis is still classical, and should stay that way

The authoritative December 2025 survey of AI for geometry preparation and meshing concludes that AI "complements established geometry and meshing algorithms rather than replacing them" ([Owen et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23719)). Sandia's design is the most transferable precedent. It predicts tet-mesh quality per geometric feature before meshing and presents a **prioritized list of suggested defeaturing operations** rather than acting unilaterally ([Sandia IMR 2019](https://www.internationalmeshingroundtable.com/assets/papers/2019/24-Owen.pdf)). The deterministic toolkit is mature. Cubit flags small curves and surfaces against length and area thresholds and applies virtual composites that "modify an overlay topology" without touching the CAD ([Cubit docs](https://www.sandia.gov/files/cubit/15.8/help_manual/WebHelp/item/clean_up/small_details.htm)). Epsilon-shapes detect thin features in both an object and its complement, which covers thin walls and narrow fluid gaps alike ([Livesu et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1704.08049)). The gmsh/OCC pipeline also has real traps an agent must learn to handle. In one reported case, calling `healShapes` before `removeAllDuplicates` on a three-solid STEP failed with "Could not make solid," while skipping healing gave a conformal mesh ([gmsh issue #2847](https://gitlab.onelab.info/gmsh/gmsh/-/issues/2847)). Entities not embedded in a higher-dimensional entity mesh independently and produce non-conformal interfaces ([PyLith gmsh guide](https://pylith.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/meshing/gmsh.html)). Boolean and heal operations also renumber entity tags, so boundary-condition assignments by tag silently break.

## Repair loops, not agent org charts, drive CAE agent success, and meshing is still unverified

### The 2026 evidence favors a generic harness with feedback and examples

CFD agents began in 2024–25 as multi-agent, RAG-heavy OpenFOAM systems: MetaOpenFOAM, OpenFOAMGPT, Foam-Agent and ChatCFD. Foam-Agent's ablation already showed where the value was. Its Reviewer, an error-log repair loop, lifted execution success from about 48–57% to 86–88%, at an average of only 0.79–1.87 loops ([Foam-Agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18178v1)). A September 2026 study isolated the variables. Holding information access and repair budget equal, **a single-agent generic harness scored 96.4% against 88.2% for specialized multi-agent systems on FoamBench**. Removing execution-feedback repair dropped it to 71.8%, and removing solver tutorials to 80.9%. Scripted reflection added nothing measurable ([Shi & Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03718)). FEA shows the same pattern. AbaqusAgent with Claude Opus 4.6 falls from 90% to **45% without its Reviewer**, and reaches only 60% on cases outside its retrieval corpus ([AbaqusAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00138v1)). The model and the repair budget dominate. Foam-Agent's repository reports Opus 4.6 rising from 85.45% to 100% on FoamBench Basic when reviewer loops go from 10 to 25. That is self-reported, and the README calls it "a benchmark result, not a guarantee" ([Foam-Agent GitHub](https://github.com/csml-rpi/Foam-Agent)).

### "It ran" is half the truth

Every headline number measures executability. Under correctness gating, success roughly halves. Foam-Agent's 88.2% on FoamBench becomes 34% in CFDLLMBench's accuracy-weighted Success Rate, and 41.8% under a stricter completion criterion ([CFDLLMBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20374); [arXiv 2505.04997](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04997)). FEABench, built on COMSOL tutorials, remains essentially unsolved under strict correctness. The best multi-turn agent reached 0.88 executability but only 2 of 15 valid targets, and its one strict pass was a false match ([FEABench](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06260)). Cross-paper numbers conflict sharply. ChatCFD reports MetaOpenFOAM at 6.2%, while MetaOpenFOAM 2.0 reports 86.9% on its own benchmark ([ChatCFD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02019)). Self-built benchmarks therefore cannot rank systems.

### Meshing novel geometry is the shared weak stage

**No paper or benchmark is dedicated to LLM-driven meshing.** Meshing appears only as a sub-step, and every documented failure is one that logs never flag. In the Peking University coding-agent study, MiniMax-M2.1 produced meshes that omitted the obstacle or reused a tutorial mesh unchanged. GPT-5.2 completed all four obstacle cases by writing Gmsh Python API code, though its hybrid mesh needed human-guided iteration, and verification was visual only ([coding agents for CFD](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11689)). AbaqusAgent misrepresented an open hole until the prompt specified a radial mesh, and GPT-5.2 hallucinated a square section for a circular rod ([AbaqusAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00138v1)). CFDLLMBench attributes Foam-Agent's mismatched doubleSquare mesh to weak spatial reasoning ([CFDLLMBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20374)). Foam-Agent added a Gmsh-script path because native OpenFOAM meshing "could not capture" obstacles, but it describes no checkMesh verification ([Foam-Agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18178v1)). FeaGPT drives Gmsh from an LLM-extracted parameter dictionary in a single pass, with no iteration ([FeaGPT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21993)). ALL-FEM explicitly scopes out "robust automated meshing or tagging" ([ALL-FEM](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.21011v2)). **None of these systems reports quantitative mesh-quality metrics or a convergence study.**

Commercial products are no further along on verification. Ansys 2026 R1's exploratory Mesh Agent "helps engineers debug and resolve meshing failures," with no performance metrics ([Ansys 2026 R1](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/synopsys-launches-ansys-2026-r1-to-re-engineer-engineering-with-joint-solutions-and-ai-powered-products-302711215.html)). SimScale's September 2026 Onshape agent prepares CAD, meshes, sets physics and runs simulations from one prompt, without published accuracy or self-verification claims ([SimScale](https://www.simscale.com/press/simscale-launches-engineering-ai-agent-for-onshape/)). Non-LLM ML meshing offers one reusable pattern: a learned sizing field driving an off-the-shelf mesher, as in AMBER's GNN imitation of expert sizing fields ([AMBER](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.14161v1)). These models are trained on 2D or narrow families, so deterministic meshers should stay the backbone.

## A blueprint: STEP as source of truth, mesh as code, tests as hooks

### What transfers from coding harnesses

Coding agents improved mainly through the interface they work through. SWE-agent's ablations quantify this. Removing the edit command cost 7.7 points of an 18.0% baseline, and removing the lint gate on edits cost 3.0. An iterative, page-through search tool was *worse than no search tool* (12.0% vs 15.7%), and collapsing old observations helped ([SWE-agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15793v3)). Anthropic's guidance follows the same lines. Build a few consolidated, poka-yoke tools rather than thin API wrappers. Return human-readable names instead of IDs, since resolving IDs reduced hallucinations. Cap and paginate outputs, and write errors that say how to fix the problem ([Writing tools for agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents)). There is a counter-trend. mini-swe-agent scores above 74% on SWE-bench Verified with bash as its only tool, and CodeAct found executable Python actions beat JSON actions by up to 20% across 17 LLMs ([mini-swe-agent](https://github.com/SWE-agent/mini-swe-agent); [CodeAct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01030)). Together the two lines of evidence mark out the harness's real job. **Let the model do generic work in a Python sandbox, and spend engineering effort on domain verifiers, queries and renderers it cannot build reliably itself.** Claude Code's own documentation names the remaining principle: "Claude stops when the work looks done. If it has nothing to run that tells it whether the work actually is done, 'looks done' is all it has to go on." Anything that must happen every time belongs in a hook, not in CLAUDE.md ([Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). The Claude Agent SDK exposes that same loop, hooks, subagents and MCP for building such a harness directly ([Agent SDK](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/overview)).

### The geometry understanding layer

The harness should never put raw STEP in the context window. On import, an OCCT-based layer (OCP via CadQuery or build123d, or pythonocc) reads the file, heals it, and produces a **geometry digest**. The digest is the CAD analogue of Aider's repo map, which ranks files and symbols and fits them into a token budget ([Aider repo map](https://aider.chat/docs/repomap.html)). It lists solids and assembly structure with volumes, bounding boxes, units and a declared coordinate frame. It aggregates faces by surface type with areas and radii. It lists recognized features (holes, fillets, chamfers, bosses, slots), with repeated instances collapsed into patterns ("8 × Ø6.6 through-holes on a Ø80 PCD"), because repeated entities are where models fail ([OmniCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22637)). It ends with a meshing hazard report built on Cubit-style small-feature and sliver thresholds relative to target element size, minimum wall thickness and gap width via epsilon-shape or ray-cast analysis, and near-tangent face pairs. Details stay behind just-in-time queries rather than in the digest, following Anthropic's context-engineering guidance ([context engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)).

Face selection must be **predicate-based and bound to names**. The model asks for "planar faces with outward normal +X and area > 100 mm²" or "cylindrical faces of radius < 2 mm adjacent to face `flange_top`." The tool answers with readable handles and computed attributes. The model then binds a semantic name (`inlet`, `fixed_support`, `bolt_holes`) to the predicate, not to an integer tag. The harness re-resolves names after every boolean, heal or defeature, using OCCT `Modified()`/`Generated()` history where available and geometric signatures (centroid, area, normal) as a fallback. This addresses the 21.51%-F1 grounding failure and gmsh's tag renumbering together. Learned labelers (UV-Net, BRepNet, AAGNet, or a few-shot model such as FeatureFox) can feed the digest as **soft hints with confidences**, given their documented brittleness. Optional CadQuery reconstruction (e.g. CADENA or cadrille on a tessellation) gives the model a compact, code-shaped feature narrative, which suits it best given the 0.84-vs-0.59 QA gap. It is accepted only when its IoU against the source B-rep clears a threshold, and the B-rep is never replaced for meshing.

### Mesh as code

The textual mesh representation should be a **re-runnable gmsh Python script plus a declarative mesh spec**, not a .msh dump. LLMs already write the gmsh API competently. GPT-5.2 succeeded through it where a weaker model's tutorial-copying failed, and Foam-Agent and FeaGPT both route custom geometry through Gmsh scripts ([coding agents for CFD](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11689); [Foam-Agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18178v1); [FeaGPT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21993)). The gmsh API covers what the script needs: STEP import and healing, Box/Distance/MathEval size fields, curvature-based sizing, element-quality queries and duplicate-node removal ([Gmsh manual](https://gmsh.info/doc/texinfo/gmsh.html)). The spec, a JSON file the agent reads but cannot edit, holds the requirements: physics type, element type and order, target and minimum sizes, boundary-layer parameters, required named groups, quality thresholds and an element budget. This mirrors Anthropic's long-running-harness pattern of a JSON feature list that starts all-failing and may not be edited ([long-running harnesses](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)). The script should follow CADENA's lesson: its DSL is built so that every program prefix executes ([CADENA](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00799)). Structure the script as named stages (import, heal, defeature, fragment or fluid-extract, name groups, size fields, generate, optimize, export), each executable and checkable on its own. The agent can then bisect failures and the harness can cache stage outputs. The script and spec together give diffable, reviewable, version-controlled "mesh source," which is the property that makes code harnesses work.

### Tools, kept few and verification-heavy

| Tool (namespaced) | What it does | Returns (bounded) |
|---|---|---|
| `geom_load` | Import STEP, heal with recorded options, fragment multi-body | Digest, heal deltas (volume/area change), validity report |
| `geom_query` | Predicate search over faces, edges and solids | ≤50 matches with names, type, area, centroid, normal or radius; "refine query" message beyond that |
| `geom_hazards` | Small edges/faces, slivers, thin walls, gaps, small radii relative to target size | Ranked hazards with locations and suggested actions (Sandia-style prioritized list) |
| `geom_name` | Bind semantic name ↔ predicate; re-resolved after every operation | Resolved entity set, warnings if resolution changed |
| `geom_edit` | Guarded defeature, virtual composite, split or fluid-volume extraction | Accept or roll back, based on volume/Hausdorff delta and the continued existence of named groups |
| `mesh_run` | Execute the staged gmsh script in an isolated worker with a timeout | Stage status, `getLastError`, element counts, quality summary |
| `mesh_inspect` | Quality histograms and worst-N elements, per group and region | Percentiles of SICN/SJ/gamma, worst element locations, mapped to nearest named face |
| `mesh_render` | Fixed canonical views, sections and worst-element close-ups, coloured by quality or group | PNG plus paired numeric caption |
| `solver_check` | checkMesh, CalculiX/dolfinx dry run or short smoke solve | Parsed pass/fail per check, thresholds exceeded |
| `mesh_converge` | Three-level refinement and GCI on a target quantity | Apparent order, GCI, pass/fail |
| `examples_search` | Retrieve working scripts and cases for similar parts or physics | Top-k snippets with provenance |

Each design choice in the table comes from an evidence base above. Search is capped at 50 results because SWE-agent found unbounded paging harmful ([SWE-agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15793v3)). Tools take names instead of tags because resolving IDs reduced hallucinations ([Writing tools for agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents)). Examples are retrieved because tutorials were the largest single gain in the CAE harness study ([Shi & Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03718)). Execution is isolated because gmsh and OCCT segfaults would otherwise kill long loops; OFFTECH's gmsh MCP server already uses isolated workers and revision guards for exactly this, including a `worker_epoch` field because tags change after recovery ([OFFTECH gmsh-mcp](https://github.com/OFFTECH/gmsh-mcp-server)).

### A mesh test suite enforced by a stop hook

The verification ladder should run cheapest-first, with hard gates enforced deterministically. In Claude Code terms, a `Stop` hook that exits with code 2 sends the agent back to work with the failure text ([Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)).

| Gate | Check | Threshold / source | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 Geometry validity | Shape valid after import and heal; heal changed volume/area by < ε | Project-set ε; heal-order sensitivity documented in [gmsh #2847](https://gitlab.onelab.info/gmsh/gmsh/-/issues/2847) | Hard |
| G2 Geometric fidelity | Volume, area and bounding box of meshed domain vs source; Hausdorff distance of boundary | Project-set tolerances; the same check catches the missing-obstacle failures in [2602.11689](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11689) | Hard |
| G3 Named groups | Every required group in the spec exists, is non-empty and resolves to the intended faces | Spec-defined | Hard |
| G4 Generation | Mesh completes, `getLastError` empty, conformal interfaces, no duplicate nodes | [Gmsh manual](https://gmsh.info/doc/texinfo/gmsh.html); [PyLith guide](https://pylith.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/meshing/gmsh.html) | Hard |
| G5 Element validity | min SICN > 0 (negative means inverted) | [CalculiX forum](https://calculix.discourse.group/t/mesh-quality-check/743) | Hard |
| G6 Element quality | CFD: non-orthogonality < 70°, skewness < 4, aspect ratio < 1000 (checkMesh); sHM controls maxNonOrtho 65, minDeterminant 0.001. Fluent practice: orthogonal quality > 0.1, skewness < 0.9 | [checkMesh source](https://cpp.openfoam.org/v13/applications_2utilities_2mesh_2manipulation_2checkMesh_2checkMesh_8C_source.html); [meshQualityDict](https://doc.openfoam.com/2306/tools/pre-processing/mesh/generation/snappyhexmesh/meshquality); [Ansys forum](https://innovationspace.ansys.com/forum/?p=327234) | Hard, with percentile reporting |
| G7 Budget | Element count within spec | Spec-defined | Hard |
| G8 Solver acceptance | Target solver reads the mesh; smoke solve runs | checkMesh / CalculiX dry run | Hard |
| G9 Discretization | Three grids, r > 1.3, GCI on target QoI | [Celik et al. 2008 / ASME JFE](https://qa12.asme.org/wwwasmeorg/media/ResourceFiles/Shop/Journals/JFENumAccuracy.pdf) | Hard when requested |
| G10 Engineering judgment | Boundary-layer adequacy, refinement in the right places, defeaturing justified | Fresh-context reviewer subagent | Soft |

The soft gate uses a reviewer subagent that "never saw your conversation." It is prompted to skip style, because "a reviewer that's asked to find problems will always find something" ([Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). Anthropic's long-running-harness work names the failure to guard against: agents declare victory early, and tests must never be removed or edited ([long-running harnesses](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)). The spec and the gate scripts must therefore sit outside the agent's write permissions.

### Visual feedback, used as a localizer rather than a judge

Renders help 3D agents, but VLMs are weak verifiers. BlenderGym's best VLM verifier agreed with humans 0.66 of the time, against 0.79 between humans. Pairwise before/after comparison and scaled verification beat absolute judgment ([BlenderGym](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01786v1)). The tool-side evidence is encouraging but self-reported: build123d-mcp's measure-render-validate loop reportedly lifted a model's CADGenBench score from 0.360 to 0.457 and validity from 88% to 100% ([build123d-mcp](https://github.com/pzfreo/build123d-mcp)). The harness should therefore render specific, deterministic views: fixed iso and axis views, surfaces coloured by named group to confirm boundary assignment, quality-coloured sections through volume meshes, and close-ups of the worst elements. Every image comes with a numeric caption so the model never reads numbers from pixels. For defeaturing and parameter choices, show the candidates side by side. Screenshots should be off by default for intermediate steps, as FreeCAD MCP's `--only-text-feedback` switch allows, and old images should be cleared from context ([FreeCAD MCP](https://github.com/neka-nat/freecad-mcp)).

## Six gaps the harness will have to fill itself

The first gap is representation. **No controlled study compares raw STEP, JSON face-adjacency graphs, natural-language digests and CadQuery for LLM reasoning about existing parts.** The digest design above is a well-supported bet, not a measured optimum. A small internal experiment would settle it for this project: the same quantitative questions (wall thickness, hole count, which faces bound the fluid) asked across serializations and models. Scaling is related and also unstudied. No source addresses summarizing 1,000+ face assemblies hierarchically within a context budget.

The second gap is simulation semantics. Feature datasets label machining operations. None labels inlets, outlets, walls, load faces or thin-wall regions, and no ML method for automatic fluid-volume or boundary identification turned up. The harness will have to bootstrap these labels from its own accepted runs, which favors sample-efficient labelers.

The third gap is persistent naming under edits. FutureCAD measures selection but not identity across parametric changes ([FutureCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11831v2)). BenchCAD's 64% silent-corruption rate shows the stakes. No source addresses tag stability in gmsh. The name-resolution layer is new engineering, not borrowed research.

The fourth gap is evaluation. No meshing benchmark exists. FoamBench-Advanced (16 cases) and FEABench Gold (15) are too small to separate strong systems, and both reward executability or downstream fields rather than mesh fidelity ([CFDLLMBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20374); [FEABench](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06260)). The project should build its own benchmark from real third-party STEP files. It should score G2–G9 pass rates, Hausdorff fidelity, group completeness, y+ attainment, GCI and cost, and report correctness-gated numbers from day one.

The fifth gap is the sourcing of thresholds. FEA scaled-Jacobian cutoffs, y+ targets per turbulence model, and official Fluent quality limits were not found in primary sources. The gate values for G6 in structural meshes are project decisions until they are documented.

The sixth gap is advanced geometry. Sweeps, lofts, threads, springs and gears break both generation and reconstruction ([Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1); [CADENA](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00799)). Fortunately, meshing does not need to regenerate them, only to recognize and simplify them. Part-level classification and replacement, as Sandia did for bolts and springs, is the practical answer. No open, maintained tool for it was found.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. Teaching a model to "see" CAD is the wrong target. The literature shows that frontier models already reason well over explicit, executable text, and fail precisely when asked to infer geometry from pixels or to pick B-rep entities unaided. A meshing harness should therefore turn every geometric question into a deterministic query. It should turn every meshing decision into diffable code and every acceptance criterion into a test the agent cannot edit. CAD-as-code earns its place as the model's *reading* medium, not as the geometry of record. The coding-harness playbook transfers almost directly once "tests" mean geometric fidelity and element quality instead of unit tests. The deepest lesson from the 2026 CAE-agent results is that orchestration complexity has stopped paying. What pays is the repair budget, the examples the model can copy, and the verifier that makes "done" checkable.

That also locates the opportunity. Published agents and 2026 commercial products alike measure whether a solver ran, not whether the mesh was faithful, valid and converged. A harness whose central artifact is a gated, benchmarked mesh test suite would be the first in the literature to make meshing correctness measurable. Its own benchmark of real third-party STEP files would be a publishable contribution in itself, because no such benchmark exists.
