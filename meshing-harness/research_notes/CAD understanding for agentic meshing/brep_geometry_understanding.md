# How ML models and LLMs/VLMs understand existing CAD geometry (B-rep, STEP, meshes, point clouds), with a view to a geometry-understanding layer for an FEA/CFD meshing agent

Scope note: these notes come from about 18 search and fetch calls made on 2026-10-30. Items tagged **[unverified]** were not confirmed against the primary paper's tables in this session. That includes snippets from search summaries, third-party pages, and abstracts whose numbers I did not see. Items tagged **[model knowledge]** come from the researcher's background knowledge. Their URLs are canonical and well known but were not fetched this session.

## 1. B-rep learning: what UV-Net, BRepNet, AAGNet, BRep-BERT, SolidGen/BrepGen, and the MFCAD-family models recognize, and how accurately

### Takeaway
Native B-rep encoders reach near-ceiling face-level accuracy (about 99%+) on synthetic machining-feature benchmarks such as MFCAD, MFCAD++ and MFInstSeg. They do much worse on realistic "how was this face made" segmentation (about 90% accuracy and about 66-77% IoU on Fusion 360 Gallery). A 2026 study reports that these encoders "collapse catastrophically" when the same solid is re-exported or repartitioned into different faces. Benchmark accuracy therefore overstates how well they hold up on arbitrary customer STEP files.

### Cited Findings
**Architectures and what they encode**
- UV-Net (Jayaraman et al., CVPR 2021; arXiv 2006.10211) samples each face's surface on a UV grid and each edge's curve on a 1D grid, encodes these with CNNs, and passes messages over the face-adjacency graph. It reports 99.95% accuracy and 99.87% IoU on MFCAD face segmentation — [search summary of UV-Net / BRepNet tables](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2006.10211v2) **[unverified numbers]**.
- BRepNet (Lambourne et al., CVPR 2021; arXiv 2104.00706) does topological message passing over oriented coedges ("winged-edge" kernels). It introduced the Fusion 360 Gallery segmentation dataset, which labels each face with the modeling operation that created it (extrude side/end, cut side/end, fillet, chamfer, revolve side/end). Its best configuration reports about 92.5% per-face accuracy and 77.1% IoU, with about a quarter of the parameters of PointNet++ and MeshCNN, and it beats an ECC graph network — [BRepNet (deepai mirror)](https://deepai.org/publication/brepnet-a-topological-message-passing-system-for-solid-models); [arXiv 2104.00706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00706v1) **[numbers via search summary]**.
- AAGNet (Wu et al., 2024, Robotics and Computer-Integrated Manufacturing) is a GNN over a geometric attributed adjacency graph that does semantic, instance and bottom-face segmentation of machining features together. It introduced **MFInstSeg**, a dataset of more than 60,000 STEP files with machining-feature instance labels. The authors say it beats the state of the art on MFCAD, MFCAD++ and MFInstSeg in both accuracy and complexity — [search summary / peeref listing](https://peeref.com/works/34020166) **[exact MFInstSeg numbers not seen]**.
- BRT ("Bringing Attention to CAD: Boundary Representation Learning via Transformer", arXiv 2504.07134, 2025) gives a head-to-head on Fusion 360 Gallery segmentation: BRT 90.47% accuracy / 70.16% IoU, UV-Net 89.03 / 66.47, BRepNet 90.19 / 68.92, AAGNet 75.53% accuracy / 82.45% IoU. AAGNet's accuracy and IoU ordering is unusual here, so check the table. On MFCAD++, AAGNet reaches about 99.29% feature-recognition accuracy and 98.64% IoU, slightly ahead of BRT (99.26%). The BRT authors attribute this to AAGNet's handling of planar faces, which make up most of MFCAD++ — [BRT arXiv 2504.07134](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07134) **[via search summary]**.
- BRepFormer (Dai et al., ACM ICMR 2025; arXiv 2504.07378, v3 June 2025) is a transformer that fuses geometric and topological features and adds an edge- and topology-derived attention bias in each layer. It claims state of the art on MFInstSeg, MFTRCAD and its new **CBF (Complex B-rep Feature) dataset of 20,000 B-reps**, which the authors describe as closer to industrial complexity — [arXiv 2504.07378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07378) (the abstract page gives no numbers).
- FeatureFox (arXiv 2604.26770, April 2026) does panoptic graph segmentation for machining features. On full MFInstSeg training data, AAGNet edges it out on panoptic quality (PQ), while FeatureFox is slightly better on feature-level recognition and localization. FeatureFox's main claim is sample efficiency: **PQ > 0.9 with about 250 training parts, versus about 5,000 for AAGNet** — [FeatureFox arXiv 2604.26770](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.26770) **[via search summary]**.
- BRep-BERT adapts BERT to B-reps. It builds entity-level token sequences from structural relationships and pre-trains with masked entity modeling plus sub-graph node contrastive learning. It reports state of the art on full-data and few-shot B-rep tasks, with the largest gains in few-shot settings. Code is announced at github.com/louyz1026/Brep_Bert — [third-party abstract (Bohrium)](https://www.bohrium.com/scholar/n11852037/Xueyang_Li) **[original paper not retrieved]**. Related self-supervised work includes Jones et al., "Self-Supervised Representation Learning for CAD" (CVPR 2023), which pre-trains on unlabeled B-rep faces with a hybrid implicit/explicit surface representation and improves few-shot segmentation — [arXiv 2210.10807](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.10807). Other 2026 entries are BRepMAE (masked graph autoencoder for machining features) and Brep2Shape — [arXiv 2603.14927](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.14927) **[via search snippet]**.
- Hierarchical CADNet (Colligan et al., Queen's University Belfast) uses a hierarchical B-rep graph: a face-adjacency level plus a mesh-facet level, processed by two spatial GCNs. It was built **explicitly for automatic feature recognition to support defeaturing in CAD-to-analysis pre-processing**, and this line of work also released MFCAD++ — [QUB thesis "Deep learning for boundary representation CAD models"](https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/studentTheses/deep-learning-for-boundary-representation-cad-models/).
- SolidGen (Jayaraman et al., 2022, arXiv 2203.13944) and BrepGen (Xu et al., SIGGRAPH 2024, arXiv 2401.15563) are **generative** models. SolidGen uses an autoregressive "indexed B-rep" (vertex/edge/face index lists). BrepGen uses a structured latent tree of faces, edges and vertices with diffusion. Their representations are useful templates for tokenizing B-reps, but neither is a recognition model — [model knowledge; arXiv 2203.13944, 2401.15563].

**Robustness to real-world B-rep variation (critical for an agent ingesting arbitrary STEP)**
- "Learn the Solid, Not the File: Canonical Inputs for Neural Networks on CAD Boundary Representations" (arXiv 2609.11573, Sept 2026) points out that one solid can have many B-reps depending on modeling choices, kernel rebuilds and export settings. It reports that "popular B-rep encoders often collapse catastrophically" under standard-benchmark perturbations, natural CAD-software variation, and a FreeCAD human-modeling dataset. Its fix is a canonical region graph whose nodes, features and coordinate frame come from the solid itself, with invariance guarantees for repartitioning and rigid motion. It matches the strongest baseline on standard benchmarks and stays stable under every perturbation tested — [arXiv 2609.11573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11573). A search snippet shows MFCAD++ values of BRepNet 0.9842 and UV-Net 0.9770 in that paper, with sharp degradation under face flipping and solid perturbations **[column semantics unverified]**.

**Datasets**
- MFCAD (Cao et al., 2020): synthetic prismatic parts, 16 machining-feature classes, face-level labels. MFCAD++: about 59,665 parts and 25 classes. AgentsCAD's abstract confirms the 59,665 figure — [arXiv 2607.02448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02448). MFInstSeg: more than 60k STEP files with instance labels (AAGNet). CBF: 20k complex B-reps (BRepFormer). Fusion 360 Gallery segmentation: about 35k real user-designed parts labeled by modeling operation (BRepNet). ABC dataset (Koch et al., CVPR 2019): about 1M Onshape models, unlabeled for semantics, mainly used for pre-training and surface-patch/sharp-edge tasks **[model knowledge for MFCAD class counts, Fusion 360 count and ABC]**.

### Inferences
- The recognized label sets (holes, slots, pockets, steps, chamfers, fillets, bosses, blind/through features, and modeling-op labels) map well onto **defeaturing candidates for meshing**: fillets, chamfers, small holes, engraved text and bosses. They do not directly encode meshing-relevant properties such as thickness, flow-path role, or whether a feature affects stress concentration.
- Accuracy on MFCAD-style data is saturated (above 99%) and is not a useful discriminator. Fusion 360 Gallery (about 90% accuracy, about 70% IoU) and robustness studies such as 2609.11573 are more realistic signals. For production, the agent should pair rule-based geometric checks (exact, kernel-derived) with learned classifiers, and treat learned labels as soft hints.
- Sample-efficient methods (FeatureFox; few-shot gains from BRep-BERT and Jones et al.) matter because labeled meshing-specific data, such as "defeature this?" or "boundary-layer surface?", will be scarce.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve exact AAGNet MFInstSeg numbers, CADNet (original, Colligan 2020) numbers, or BRep-BERT's paper tables.
- No found benchmark labels faces with **simulation semantics** such as inlet/outlet/wall, load/BC surfaces, or thin-wall regions. This looks like an open gap.

## 2. Serializing B-rep and STEP geometry into text or tokens for LLMs, and which serialization supports reasoning best

### Takeaway
Three approaches appear in the literature: (a) learned B-rep encoders projected into the LLM's token space (BrepLLM, BrepCoder); (b) structured text or JSON face summaries with adjacency, built by a CAD kernel (AgentsCAD-style); and (c) raw STEP text or CAD code (STEP-LLM, mostly for generation). **I found no controlled study comparing text serializations, or comparing raw STEP with structured summaries, for LLM reasoning.** The available evidence is indirect: raw STEP is impractical, LLMs "cannot perceive B-rep geometry" when asked to pick primitives, and kernel-computed summaries are what working agent systems use.

### Cited Findings
- **BrepLLM** (Deng et al., arXiv 2512.16413; v2 dated 29 June 2026). B-reps are **not** fed as text. Adaptive UV sampling builds a face-adjacency graph. A hierarchical BrepEncoder produces 128-dimensional node tokens from face, edge-conditioned and global-topology features, plus a global token that is CLIP-aligned with a frozen text encoder. An MLP projects the tokens to a Q-Former (32 queries), which feeds Qwen3-8B with LoRA. The paper introduces **Brep2Text**: 269,444 QA pairs over 134,722 B-reps built from the Text2CAD corpus, with questions generated by Qwen-Max and a 200-model test set. Captioning (Qwen3-30B judge) scores 61.36 against 56.58 for MiniGPT-3D (point cloud). Human precision is 83.62 and hallucination 0.83. Classification averages 60.0% against 56.25%. Baselines, all retrained on Brep2Text, were LLaVA-13B and Qwen3-VL-8B (multi-view images), PointLLM-7B/13B, ShapeLLM-7B/13B and MiniGPT-3D (point clouds). The authors conclude that native B-rep modeling beats point-cloud and multi-view inputs. **There is no text-serialization baseline**, and all results are self-reported on the authors' own dataset with an LLM judge — [arXiv 2512.16413](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16413).
- **BrepCoder** is described as a unified MLLM that performs diverse CAD tasks from B-rep inputs. It argues that prior multimodal CAD work focuses on point clouds or images rather than industry-standard B-rep — [arXiv 2602.22284](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.22284) **[via search summary only]**.
- **FutureCAD / BRepGround** (arXiv 2603.11831, 2026) tests text-based B-rep primitive grounding, i.e. choosing the faces or edges a natural-language instruction refers to. The LLM-based approach reaches **only 21.51% F1**, which the authors attribute to "limited selector expressiveness and the inability of LLMs to perceive B-Rep geometry" — [arXiv 2603.11831](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11831v2) **[via search summary]**. This is the most direct evidence that LLMs struggle to identify specific B-rep entities from text alone.
- **AgentsCAD** (George, Keefe, Pak, Barati Farimani, CMU; arXiv 2607.02448, July 2026) is an agentic DfM system. It parses STEP, detects overhangs with a 45° threshold, builds a face-adjacency topology graph, optionally adds GraphSAGE labels trained on MFCAD++, passes the result to a Claude Sonnet reasoning agent, and uses a GPT-4o VLM verifier on rendered views. Per the search summary, the authors state that raw STEP "encodes thousands of faces, edges, and vertices" in a form LLMs "cannot parse directly". They use CadQuery to expose **per-face summaries (surface type, area, normal, centroid) plus adjacency**, and say adjacency lets the LLM infer features such as "a planar face sharing an edge with a cylindrical face is a candidate through-hole". **The only evaluation is a qualitative birdhouse case study**, and the authors describe the problem as only "partially solved" — [arXiv 2607.02448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02448).
- **STEP-LLM** (arXiv 2601.12641, Jan 2026) generates STEP text directly from natural language. Outputs are evaluated by converting to point clouds and computing Chamfer distance, with 95.18% renderability reported. The authors note there are no widely adopted text-to-CAD benchmarks — [arXiv 2601.12641](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.12641) **[via search summary]**. This shows LLMs can emit valid STEP syntax. It says nothing about reasoning over existing STEP.
- **B-repLer** (arXiv 2508.10201, 2025) does language-guided editing of B-rep CAD models — [arXiv 2508.10201](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.10201) **[not read; relevant as a B-rep+language interface]**.
- **NURBGen** (arXiv 2511.06194) has an LLM emit NURBS surface parameters as structured text — [arXiv 2511.06194](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.06194) **[not read]**.
- **BRep-BERT** tokenizes B-rep entities into discrete sequences for masked modeling. A 2026 MAE paper cites it as converting B-rep geometry and topology "into a limited number of token IDs" — [arXiv 2603.14927](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.14927) **[via snippet]**.
- **SolidGen's indexed B-rep** (vertex list, then edges as vertex indices, then faces as edge indices) is the main precedent for a compact, LLM-friendly topological tokenization — [model knowledge; arXiv 2203.13944].

### Inferences
- For a meshing agent built on a frontier LLM without fine-tuning, the most defensible choice is a **kernel-computed structured summary**, not raw STEP. Raw STEP is long, uses indirection through entity #IDs, and the LLM would have to do the geometry math itself. A good summary would contain per-solid volume and bounding box. Per face: type, area, normal or axis, radius, centroid, and adjacency with dihedral convexity. It would also add derived features such as holes, fillets, thin regions and small edges. This mirrors AgentsCAD and the attributed adjacency graph inputs used by AAGNet.
- Since LLMs score poorly at selecting B-rep primitives from text (21.51% F1), the agent should **not ask the LLM to compute geometry**. It should query deterministic tools ("list faces with area < X", "min wall thickness", "faces with radius < r") and reason over their outputs. Stable face IDs in the serialization are essential (see Q5 on persistent naming).
- Learned B-rep tokens (BrepLLM style) need fine-tuning and are currently validated only on captioning and classification, not on quantitative meshing questions such as dimensions or thickness.

### Gaps
- **No controlled comparison** was found of raw STEP vs JSON face-adjacency vs natural-language summaries vs CAD-code (CadQuery) for LLM question answering on existing parts. A project could run this experiment itself.
- No evidence was found on how many faces fit into a useful LLM context or how to summarize hierarchically for 1,000+ face assemblies.

## 3. VLM/LLM CAD question-answering and spatial-reasoning benchmarks, and how frontier models do

### Takeaway
Frontier VLMs are mediocre at understanding 3D mechanical parts from images: about 42-61% on a small multiple-choice set in 2024. Recent benchmarks (MechVQA, OmniCAD, BenchCAD, all 2026) consistently find spatial relations, repeated parts and metric reasoning to be the weak points. Structured side information, such as giving the model a mate graph, helps substantially.

### Cited Findings
- **CAD-VQA** (Taghanaki, Lambourne, Mongkhounsavath, Autodesk; arXiv 2409.02253, 2024): 17 3D mechanical parts and 85 multiple-choice questions on part names, geometric features, assembly features and function. Each question comes with a composite of 6 rendered views. Accuracy: **Claude-3.5-Sonnet 61%, GPT-4o 54%, Gemini-1.5-Pro 54%, o1-preview 42%**. The accompanying paper is about finding the rendering style ("image distribution") a black-box VLM prefers — [GitHub asgsaeid/cad_vqa](https://github.com/asgsaeid/cad_vqa).
- **CADTalk** (Yuan et al., CVPR 2024; arXiv 2311.16703) does semantic commenting of CAD programs: it splits the code into blocks that correspond to shape parts and labels them. The method executes the code, renders multi-view photorealistic images, applies foundation segmentation/vision models, and maps results back to code. The benchmark has 5,288 machine-made and 45 human-made programs, with **83.24% accuracy** reported. It shows a multi-view rendering route to part semantics — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Yuan_CADTalk_An_Algorithm_and_Benchmark_for_Semantic_Commenting_of_CAD_CVPR_2024_paper.html); [project page](https://enigma-li.github.io/CADTalk).
- **MechVQA** (arXiv 2605.30794, 2026): about 3.3K real mechanical part and assembly drawings with about 21K QA pairs, organized under Recognition, Reasoning and Judging with 10 subtasks. It reports that MLLMs rely on unreliable spatial-relation reasoning — [arXiv 2605.30794](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30794v1) **[per-model numbers not retrieved]**.
- **OmniCAD** (arXiv 2608.22637, 2026): 25K real mechanical assemblies, testing component-level 3D spatial reasoning, part-to-part relational reasoning and tool-augmented agentic reasoning. Accuracy at identifying **non-repeated parts is often above 90% but drops to about 26% for repeated parts**. **Supplying the mate graph significantly improved pose estimation** — [arXiv 2608.22637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22637) **[via search summary]**.
- **BenchCAD** (arXiv 2605.10865, 2026): an industry-standard programmatic-CAD benchmark covering visual QA, code QA, code generation and editing, with four capability levels running from holistic visual recognition to spatial and code reasoning — [arXiv 2605.10865](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10865v1) **[results not retrieved]**.
- **SIRI-Bench** (arXiv 2506.14512, 2025) is not CAD-specific. VLMs consistently fail to solve more than 50% of problems when spatial information must be inferred from video — [arXiv 2506.14512](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.14512).
- **BrepLLM** baselines show 3D-LLMs applied to CAD parts: PointLLM and ShapeLLM (point clouds) and LLaVA and Qwen3-VL (multi-view) all trail the native B-rep model on Brep2Text captioning and classification after retraining. Absolute classification accuracy is only about 56-60% even for the best model — [arXiv 2512.16413](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16413).

### Inferences
- A meshing agent should not rely on a VLM's reading of renderings for anything quantitative, such as dimensions, wall thickness or "is this face an inlet". VLM views work best as a **sanity check or verifier**, as in AgentsCAD's GPT-4o verifier, and for coarse semantics ("this looks like a heat sink or manifold").
- The OmniCAD mate-graph result and the AgentsCAD design both suggest that **injecting explicit structure (graphs, computed relations) is what lifts LLM spatial reasoning**. Repeated and identical entities, such as bolt-hole patterns or fin arrays, are a known failure mode, so the serialization should explicitly group or pattern-label repeated features.

### Gaps
- No benchmark was found that tests frontier LLMs on quantitative B-rep questions over existing parts (e.g. "minimum wall thickness", "number of through holes", "which faces bound the fluid volume"). The original CAD-QA name in the brief did not resolve to a specific benchmark.
- I lack current (2026) frontier-model scores (Claude 4.x, GPT-5, Gemini 2.5/3) on MechVQA, OmniCAD and BenchCAD. These papers likely contain them but were not fetched.

## 4. Multi-view rendering vs text vs native 3D encoders: reported tradeoffs for understanding

### Takeaway
The only same-dataset comparison found (BrepLLM) favors native B-rep encoders over point clouds and multi-view images for captioning and classification. Multi-view renderings remain the zero-shot route for frontier VLMs but are weak at precise and spatial questions. Text or structured kernel summaries are exact and need no training, but had no controlled evaluation. No paper compares all three on the same understanding tasks.

### Cited Findings
- On Brep2Text, a native B-rep encoder plus LLM (BrepLLM) beat multi-view VLMs (LLaVA-13B, Qwen3-VL-8B) and point-cloud LLMs (PointLLM, ShapeLLM, MiniGPT-3D), with all models retrained on the same data. Margins were modest: 61.36 vs 56.58 for captioning and 60.0% vs 56.25% for classification — [arXiv 2512.16413](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16413).
- Multi-view rendering pipelines (CADTalk at 83.24% on semantic commenting; CAD-VQA at 42-61% for frontier VLMs) need no 3D training. The CAD-VQA authors found VLM performance depends on rendering style, which motivated their "preferred image distribution" study — [CADTalk](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Yuan_CADTalk_An_Algorithm_and_Benchmark_for_Semantic_Commenting_of_CAD_CVPR_2024_paper.html); [CAD-VQA](https://github.com/asgsaeid/cad_vqa).
- CAD-MLLM (arXiv 2411.04954), a generation model, accepts text, multi-view images or point clouds and reports its strongest topology and enclosure metrics with point-cloud conditioning — [arXiv 2411.04954](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2411.04954v1) **[via search summary; generation, not understanding]**.
- Native B-rep encoders keep exact surface type and topology but are brittle to B-rep re-partitioning, as reported in 2609.11573 — [arXiv 2609.11573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11573). Point clouds and meshes are invariant to how the B-rep is partitioned but lose exact analytic surface types, which BRepNet's comparison against PointNet++ and MeshCNN reflects — [BRepNet](https://deepai.org/publication/brepnet-a-topological-message-passing-system-for-solid-models).

### Inferences
- A practical hybrid for a meshing agent has three parts. (1) Exact kernel queries serialized as structured text are the source of truth for dimensions, thickness and topology. (2) A trained or fine-tuned B-rep GNN supplies soft feature labels (fillet, hole, chamfer, defeature candidate). (3) Multi-view renders go to the VLM for coarse intent and for verifying proposed defeaturing or boundary assignments. This matches the AgentsCAD architecture (graph, Claude reasoning, GPT-4o visual verifier).

### Gaps
- No head-to-head study was found of text-serialized B-rep vs native encoder vs multi-view on identical quantitative questions.

## 5. Geometry analysis needed for meshing: defeaturing, small features, thin regions, curvature, slivers, repair, fluid volumes, persistent naming

### Takeaway
Production meshing tools still rely mainly on classic deterministic algorithms: threshold-based small-entity detection, virtual topology composites, medial-axis or epsilon-shape thin-feature detection, and shape healing. ML is entering as a ranking or advisory layer, for example Sandia's mesh-quality prediction that prioritizes suggested CAD operations. The authoritative 2025-26 survey's stance is that AI "complements established geometry and meshing algorithms rather than replacing them."

### Cited Findings
**Surveys and ML defeaturing**
- "A Survey of AI Methods for Geometry Preparation and Mesh Generation in Engineering Simulation" (Owen, Brown, Chrisochoides, Garimella, Gu, Ledoux, Lei, Quadros, Ray, Winovich, Zhang; arXiv 2512.23719, v1 Dec 2025, v2 Jan 2026) covers part classification, segmentation, defeaturing, mesh-quality prediction, and scripting automation via RL and LLMs. Its stated stance is that AI "is beginning to reduce the manual effort in the CAD-to-mesh pipeline" and "complements established geometry and meshing algorithms rather than replacing them" — [arXiv 2512.23719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23719). This should be the anchor reference for the report.
- Sandia, "CAD Defeaturing Using Machine Learning" (Owen, Shead, Martin; IMR 2019). Models trained on geometric and topological features from the CAD model, using local mesh-quality metrics as ground truth, **predict tet-mesh quality for each geometric feature before meshing**. They present a **prioritized list of suggested geometric operations** to the user — [IMR 2019 paper (Owen)](https://www.internationalmeshingroundtable.com/assets/papers/2019/24-Owen.pdf); [Sandia publication record](https://www.sandia.gov/research/publications/details/cad-defeaturing-using-machine-learning-2019-06-01/).
- Follow-on Sandia work uses ML to **classify whole parts** (bolts, springs, etc.) so they can be simplified or replaced in one step — [OSTI 1640901](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1640901) **[via search summary]**. A later Sandia paper (Parry et al., about 2023-25) trains random-forest and neural-network models to predict mesh quality from geometric features, with an expert in the loop — [OSTI 2004848](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/2004848) **[via search summary; exact date unverified]**.
- Earlier ML defeaturing: Danglade et al. (2013) learned defeaturing rules from expert configurations, and Pernot et al. (2014) predicted how defeaturing affects heat-transfer FEA results — [Danglade 2013](https://sam.ensam.eu/bitstream/10985/8300/3/lsisinsm_CADA_danglade_2013.pdf); [Pernot 2014](https://sam.ensam.eu/bitstream/10985/11381/3/LSIS-INSM_MDEAM_Pernot_2014.pdf) (pre-2018, for context).
- Hierarchical CADNet and MFCAD++ (QUB) were explicitly motivated by automating defeaturing — [QUB thesis](https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/studentTheses/deep-learning-for-boundary-representation-cad-models/).
- ML that links mesh size to geometry includes BMO-GNN (Park & Kang, KAIST; JCDE 2024, vol. 11 no. 6; arXiv 2406.01996). It uses Bayesian optimization to choose element size for GNN surrogates on CAD-derived meshes, because overly coarse meshes lose detail and overly fine ones waste compute. **This is mesh sizing for surrogate learning, not mesh-size-field prediction for FEA** — [arXiv 2406.01996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01996v1).

**Classic small-feature and virtual-topology tooling**
- Sandia Cubit "small details" tools (ITEM) flag small curves by comparing length to a threshold, and small surfaces by comparing area to a threshold based on that length squared. They place small features into four classes. **Virtual operations** (composites, partitions) come from CGM and are independent of the solid-modeling kernel: they "modify an overlay topology on the original CAD model" without changing the CAD. "Remove topology" is the heavier alternative, which cuts out the region around small or narrow surfaces and rebuilds simplified topology — [Cubit 15.8 docs](https://www.sandia.gov/files/cubit/15.8/help_manual/WebHelp/item/clean_up/small_details.htm).
- Abaqus Virtual Topology merges small faces or edges into adjacent ones. Adding virtual topology can restrict later meshing, and medial-axis meshing is not allowed in regions with virtual topology — [Abaqus docs mirror](https://ceae-server.colorado.edu/v2016/books/usi/pt03ch17s06s06.html). COMSOL's Remove Details and Geometry Cleanup Wizard use virtual operations that let the mesher ignore vertices, edges or faces — [COMSOL 6.1 Remove Details](https://doc.comsol.com/6.1/doc/com.comsol.help.cad/cad_introduction.2.20.html); [COMSOL 6.4 Cleanup Wizard](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.cad/cad_introduction.2.05.html).

**Thin-region detection**
- The medial axis is the locus of points with more than one closest boundary point. Adding distance-to-boundary gives the Medial Axis Transform (MAT), whose radius is local half-thickness. Computing the MA/MAT of a surface mesh is expensive and the result is hard to manipulate. **Epsilon-shapes** (Livesu et al., arXiv 1704.08049, 2017, slightly pre-2018) detects thin features in both the object and its complement. That makes it relevant to **thin walls and to narrow fluid gaps or channels** — [arXiv 1704.08049](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1704.08049).

**Healing, fragmenting and fluid-domain extraction (gmsh/OCC)**
- In gmsh's OCC kernel, `removeAllDuplicates` boolean-fragments all highest-dimensional entities and removes coincident duplicates, which makes shared interfaces conformal — [gmsh-sys docs](https://docs.rs/gmsh-sys/0.1.2/gmsh_sys/fn.gmshModelOccRemoveAllDuplicates.html). A gmsh issue reports that calling `healShapes` before `removeAllDuplicates` on a three-solid STEP failed with "Could not make solid", while skipping healing produced a conformal mesh — [gmsh issue #2847](https://gitlab.onelab.info/gmsh/gmsh/-/issues/2847). The lesson is that healing order and options matter and need to be checked on each case.
- `synchronize` must be called after OCC geometry operations. Curves or surfaces not embedded in a higher-dimensional entity are meshed independently, which produces non-conformal interfaces and causes PETSc import errors — [PyLith gmsh guide](https://pylith.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/meshing/gmsh.html).
- The standard fluid-domain pattern is: bounding box or enclosure, boolean cut of the solids (or fragment and keep the cavity volume), then tag inlet/outlet/wall surfaces. Internal flow volumes are extracted by capping openings and filling — [model knowledge; standard in gmsh tutorials t16/t20 and Ansys SpaceClaim "Volume Extract"; not fetched].

**Persistent naming**
- Boolean fragment and heal operations renumber entity tags, so physical groups and BC assignments by tag become unreliable. FreeCAD's "Topological Naming Problem" is the best-known public treatment (a mitigation landed in FreeCAD 1.0, 2024). OCCT provides `BRepTools_History` / `BRepAlgoAPI_*::Modified()/Generated()` for tracking — [model knowledge; FreeCAD wiki "Topological naming problem"; OCCT docs; not fetched]. I found no source that addresses tag stability in gmsh specifically **[gap]**.

### Inferences
- The geometry-understanding layer should compute a deterministic **"meshing hazard report"** with: small edges and faces (Cubit-style thresholds relative to target element size), sliver faces (high aspect ratio, small area), short edges next to long ones, minimum wall thickness (MAT, epsilon-shapes, or ray-casting thickness on a tessellation), small fillet and hole radii, tangent or near-tangent face pairs, and gaps between solids (epsilon-shapes in the complement). An LLM or ML ranker then decides what to defeature, which is what Sandia's prioritized-suggestion design does.
- Prefer **virtual/overlay topology or mesh-level suppression** to destructive CAD edits where the mesher supports it. Track face identity through every boolean or heal using kernel history, so that the agent's semantic labels (inlet, wall, bolt hole) survive.

### Gaps
- No source was found on ML-based **mesh-size-field prediction directly from B-rep for FEA/CFD** (as opposed to surrogate sizing). The survey (2512.23719) probably covers this, but its full text was not read.
- No ML method for **automatic fluid-volume or inlet/outlet identification** was found in this session.
- No quantitative results were retrieved for Sandia's defeaturing classifiers (accuracy and speedups).

## 6. Open-source tooling for extracting these properties from STEP (OCCT/OCP, pythonocc, CadQuery, gmsh OCC, FreeCAD, Salome)

### Takeaway
Everything the summary layer needs (face types, areas, adjacency, curvature, distances and thickness, healing, fragmenting, history) is available in OpenCascade (OCCT). It is exposed in Python through pythonocc-core or OCP (which CadQuery and build123d use), and at a higher level through gmsh's OCC API and FreeCAD/Salome. Most B-rep ML codebases already use OCC-based loaders for feature extraction.

### Cited Findings
- gmsh's OCC kernel API (`gmsh.model.occ.importShapes`, `healShapes`, `fragment`, `removeAllDuplicates`, `synchronize`, plus `gmsh.model.getCurvature`, `getBoundary`, `getAdjacencies`, `getMass` / `getCenterOfMass`) supports import, healing, conformal multi-body fragmenting and basic topology queries — [gmsh-sys docs](https://docs.rs/gmsh-sys/0.1.2/gmsh_sys/fn.gmshModelOccRemoveAllDuplicates.html); [PyLith gmsh guide](https://pylith.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/meshing/gmsh.html); full API in the gmsh reference manual (gmsh.info) **[model knowledge for the function list]**.
- AgentsCAD used **CadQuery** to extract per-face surface type, area, normal and centroid plus a face-adjacency graph from STEP for an LLM agent — [arXiv 2607.02448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02448).
- Relevant OCCT classes **[model knowledge; OCCT docs at dev.opencascade.org not fetched]**:
  - `STEPControl_Reader` / `STEPCAFControl_Reader` to read STEP, including names, colors and assembly structure.
  - `TopExp::MapShapesAndAncestors` for edge-to-face adjacency.
  - `BRepAdaptor_Surface::GetType` for plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus, BSpline and other types.
  - `GProp_GProps` / `BRepGProp` for area, volume and centroid.
  - `BRepLProp_SLProps` / `GeomLProp` for curvature.
  - `BRepExtrema_DistShapeShape` for minimum distances.
  - `ShapeAnalysis_*` / `ShapeFix_*` / `ShapeUpgrade_*` (the Shape Healing toolkit) for small edges, gaps, sewing and unifying same-domain faces (`ShapeUpgrade_UnifySameDomain`).
  - `BRepCheck_Analyzer` for validity.
  - `BRepAlgoAPI_Fuse`/`Cut`/`Splitter` with `Modified`/`Generated` history for persistent naming.
- UV-Net and BRepNet ship OCC-based (pythonocc / occwl) B-rep-to-graph extraction pipelines. Autodesk's `occwl` wrapper is the common UV-grid sampler — [model knowledge; github.com/AutodeskAILab/UV-Net, BRepNet, occwl; not fetched].
- FreeCAD (Part / OCC, FEM workbench with gmsh/Netgen; topological-naming mitigation in 1.0) and Salome (GEOM module for healing, partitioning and "Check Free Boundaries/Small Edges"; SMESH for meshing) offer GUI and Python access to the same OCC capabilities **[model knowledge; not fetched]**.
- Cubit (Sandia, mostly closed/licensed; Coreform Cubit is commercial) is the reference for small-feature and virtual-topology cleanup — [Cubit docs](https://www.sandia.gov/files/cubit/15.8/help_manual/WebHelp/item/clean_up/small_details.htm).

### Inferences
- A minimal stack would be: OCP or pythonocc for exact queries and healing, `occwl` for UV-grid features (enabling a pretrained UV-Net, BRepNet or AAGNet feature labeler), a tessellation plus ray-casting or MAT library for thickness, and gmsh OCC for fragmenting, fluid-volume booleans and meshing. Kernel history maps should keep a stable face-ID table that the LLM-facing JSON refers to.

### Gaps
- No open-source tool was found that ships an off-the-shelf **thin-wall / medial-axis analysis for B-rep**. It would likely have to be built on a tessellation (e.g. epsilon-shapes-style or ray-based thickness). This was not verified against the libraries' current feature lists.
- No benchmark was found comparing OCC healing (ShapeFix) against commercial healing (CADfix, Ansys SpaceClaim) on dirty STEP.
